\begin{abstract}
Single-cue deepfake-video detectors may fail when synthesis weakens spatial artefacts or alters temporal consistency; identity overlap between data splits can also inflate estimates. NeuroPulse evaluates four architecturally distinct forensic streams. Here, ``orthogonal'' means separation of forensic mechanisms, not mathematical orthogonality or statistical independence. Stream~I derives a 117-dimensional, nine-region CHROM-rPPG descriptor for stacked classification. Stream~II combines EfficientNet-B4, a bidirectional LSTM and multi-head attention. Stream~III uses depthwise-separable Xception, Efficient Channel Attention, an auxiliary learned-feature branch and hard-example mining. Stream~IV combines Swin-Tiny, an analytic block-DCT descriptor and a packed-sequence BiLSTM. The late-fusion study examines iterative imputation, beta calibration, weight selection, temperature scaling and split-conformal prediction. On the reported corpus (1,754 videos and 1,042 recorded identity labels), the selected fusion uses rPPG, EfficientNet and Swin-Tiny, excluding Xception; it reports AUC 0.8081 (95\% CI: 0.7856--0.8293), an absolute gain of 0.020 over the Swin-only aligned-set result. This difference is not statistically significant (DeLong's test, $p{=}0.217$). The rPPG--Swin score correlation ($r{=}0.232$) is consistent with partial complementarity, not independence. The result remains exploratory pending reconciliation of score coverage and independent validation of the complete fusion-selection pipeline.
\keywords{Deepfake detection \and Remote photoplethysmography \and Swin Transformer \and Efficient Channel Attention \and DCT frequency analysis \and Late-fusion ensemble}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
GANs~\cite{goodfellow2014gan}, face swaps and diffusion models~\cite{ho2020ddpm} enable high-fidelity portrait videos and raise risks of disinformation, identity fraud and non-consensual imagery~\cite{rossler2019ff,li2020celebdf}. Spatial and temporal artefacts may become less reliable as synthesis improves, while physiological cues offer a potentially complementary signal~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher}. Identity overlap between training and evaluation can inflate performance by rewarding identity recognition rather than forgery detection.

NeuroPulse evaluates four architecturally separated cue-processing pathways: haemodynamic, convolutional spatio-temporal, auxiliary learned-feature and hierarchical-transformer/DCT. In this paper, “orthogonal” describes this architectural separation; it does not claim vector-space orthogonality, zero score correlation or statistical independence. The contributions are: (1)~a four-stream evaluation; (2)~nine-region, 117-feature CHROM-rPPG; (3)~EfficientNet-B4, Xception and Swin-Tiny with BiLSTM/attention, ECA, MixUp/CutMix, hard-example mining and a frame curriculum; (4)~a comparison of imputation, calibration, fusion weighting and conformal prediction; and (5)~dataset-specific group splitting. The optimized fusion reported below selects three streams and excludes Xception; global cross-dataset identity deduplication and nested evaluation of the complete fusion pipeline are not established by the supplied evidence.

\section{Related Work}
FaceForensics++ documents manipulation and compression effects relevant to Xception-based detection~\cite{rossler2019ff,chollet2017xception}; Celeb-DF~\cite{li2020celebdf} provides a challenging benchmark, and frequency analysis has been used to study synthesis artefacts~\cite{frank2020leveraging}. EfficientNet scales network width, depth and resolution~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}. FakeCatcher~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher}, DeepRhythm~\cite{qi2020deeprhythm}, CHROM~\cite{dehaan2013chrom} and POS~\cite{wang2017pos} investigate physiological cues. Swin supplies shifted-window attention~\cite{liu2021swin}; ECA-Net supplies lightweight channel attention~\cite{wang2020eca}; calibration and conformal methods address score reliability and uncertainty~\cite{guo2017calibration,kull2017beta,vovk2005conformal}. NeuroPulse studies these cue families in one reported fusion pipeline, but its current heterogeneous evaluation protocols do not establish superiority over external methods.

\subsection{Four-Stream Architecture and Complementarity}
NeuroPulse exploits complementary failure modes: compression may erase spatial artefacts while leaving physiological incoherence, whereas high-fidelity forgeries may defeat spatial detectors but lack coherent rPPG. Each stream $i\in\{1,\dots,4\}$ emits a fake probability $p_i\in[0,1]$ for late fusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Corpus and Identity-Disjoint Evaluation}
The consolidated corpus combines FaceForensics++~\cite{rossler2019ff} (C23: originals plus Deepfakes, Face2Face, FaceSwap, NeuralTextures, FaceShifter and DeepFakeDetection, up to 200 each), Celeb-DF~v2~\cite{li2020celebdf} (150+50 real, 200 synthesised), a DFDC sample~\cite{dolhansky2020dfdc} and a custom 400+400 corpus. Balancing, $n_{\mathrm{balanced}}=\min(|\mathcal R|,|\mathcal F|)$, gives 877 real and 877 fake videos (1,754 total) across 1,042 dataset-recorded identity labels. Dataset-specific keys (source-video number, celebrity identifier, filename or person index) support stratified group $K$-fold ($K{=}5$) evaluation within each dataset, enforcing for each fold $q$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\mathrm{ID}^{(q)}_{\mathrm{train}}\cap \mathrm{ID}^{(q)}_{\mathrm{val}}\bigr)=\emptyset ,
\label{eq:leak}
\end{equation}
verified by assertion before every run; thresholds are tuned on validation data only. These checks establish separation under dataset-local keys; no cross-dataset person-resolution or duplicate-source audit is documented, so the manuscript does not claim global identity deduplication.

\subsection{Face Localisation, Alignment and Quality Control}
Stream~I uses MediaPipe FaceMesh~\cite{kartynnik2019facemesh} (468 landmarks) on up to 60 uniformly sampled frames; Streams~II--IV use MTCNN~\cite{zhang2016mtcnn} on $T{=}16$ uniform frames, with a centre-crop fallback. For Streams~III--IV, eye landmarks define roll as $\theta=\operatorname{arctan2}(\Delta y_{\mathrm{eyes}},\Delta x_{\mathrm{eyes}})$; crops are de-rotated when $|\theta|>2^{\circ}$ to reduce pose effects. Crops whose Laplacian variance $\Var[\nabla^{2}I]$ is below 20.0 are rejected as blurry.

\section{Stream I: Haemodynamic rPPG Analysis}
Cardiac blood-volume changes produce subtle skin-colour fluctuations (remote photoplethysmography, rPPG). Potential forensic cues are (1)~\emph{spatial coherence}: the pulse wave (roughly 5--10\,m/s) reaches facial regions with structured phase delays that synthesis may disrupt; (2)~\emph{spectral structure} in the 0.7--4\,Hz band; (3)~\emph{heart-rate-variability (HRV) proxies}; and (4)~\emph{phase synchronisation}~\cite{lachaux1999plv} between regions. These are candidate cues, not universal properties of every synthetic video.

Nine convex-hull regions (forehead centre, left/right forehead, cheeks, nose, chin and jaws) provide high capillary density and bilateral symmetry. For region mask $\Omega_r$ in frame $t$, mean RGB colour is extracted and CHROM~\cite{dehaan2013chrom} decomposes reflected light into chrominance channels to suppress specular noise:
\begin{align}
\bar{\mathbf c}^{(r)}_t&=\frac{1}{|\Omega_r|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\Omega_r}\mathbf I_t(x,y),\\
X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}&=3R-2G,\qquad Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}=1.5R+G-1.5B,\\
\mathrm{BVP}(t)&=X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t)-\frac{\sigma_X}{\sigma_Y}\,Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t),
\end{align}
Here, $R,G,B$ are region-averaged RGB channels and $\sigma_X,\sigma_Y$ are sliding-window standard deviations. In the windowed implementation, mean-normalised signals $R_n,G_n,B_n$ give
\begin{align}
X_s&=3R_n-2G_n,\qquad Y_s=1.5R_n+G_n-1.5B_n,\\
\alpha_c&=\clip\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\sigma(X_s)}{\sigma(Y_s)},\,0,\,10\Bigr),\qquad P=X_s-\alpha_c Y_s,
\end{align}
Window length is $w=\max(\lfloor 1.6\,f_s\rfloor,2)$ and step $s=\max(1,\lfloor w/2\rfloor)$ (50\% overlap), with overlap-add reconstruction. The signal is detrended and passed through a 3rd-order Butterworth band-pass~\cite{butterworth1930}
\begin{equation}
\lvert H(\mathrm{j}\omega)\rvert^{2}=\Bigl[1+\Bigl(\frac{\omega^{2}-\omega_L\omega_H}{(\omega_H-\omega_L)\,\omega}\Bigr)^{2n}\Bigr]^{-1},\qquad n=3,
\end{equation}
Cutoffs are $f_L{=}0.7$\,Hz and $f_H{=}4.0$\,Hz (42--240\,BPM). Gaps under 30\% of frames are linearly interpolated; POS~\cite{wang2017pos} is the fallback, and spectra use Welch's method~\cite{welch1967} with $n_{\mathrm{perseg}}{=}\min(256,\lfloor T/2\rfloor)$ and $n_{\mathrm{fft}}{=}1024$.

The 117-dimensional descriptor includes \textbf{spectral} features (24: SNR, purity, prominence, dominant frequency, harmonic ratio, entropy, centroid and temporal statistics); \textbf{HRV and phase} (13: eight metrics including RMSSD, SDNN, pNN50 and LF/HF, plus five Hilbert phase/BPM features); \textbf{cross-ROI correlation/coherence} (18); \textbf{geometry} (26 scale-invariant landmark ratios); and \textbf{signal quality/extended bands} (36). Phase synchronisation across region pairs is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PLV}_{ab}=\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}e^{\,\mathrm{j}(\phi_a(t)-\phi_b(t))}\right|,
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm{j}^{2}=-1$ and $\phi_a(t)$ is the instantaneous Hilbert phase~\cite{lachaux1999plv}. After percentile clipping, scaling and ExtraTrees selection, XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost} (300 trees), LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} (8 leaves) and HistGradientBoosting (300 iterations) feed a logistic-regression meta-learner through 5-fold stacked generalisation~\cite{wolpert1992stacking}, trained on an identity-aware 80/20 group split.

\section{Stream II: EfficientNet-B4 Spatio-Temporal Network}
The EfficientNet-B4~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} (stochastic-depth rate 0.2; global average pooling) maps each frame $\bx_{b,t}$ to
\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_{b,t}=\text{EfficientNet-B4}(\bx_{b,t})\in\R^{1792}.
\end{equation}
A 2-layer bidirectional LSTM~\cite{hochreiter1997lstm} models temporal inconsistency (256 hidden units per direction; inter-layer dropout 0.5):
\begin{align}
\overrightarrow{\bh}_t&=\mathrm{LSTM}_{\mathrm{fwd}}(\mathbf f_t,\overrightarrow{\bh}_{t-1}),\quad
\overleftarrow{\bh}_t=\mathrm{LSTM}_{\mathrm{bwd}}(\mathbf f_t,\overleftarrow{\bh}_{t+1}),\\
\bh_t&=[\overrightarrow{\bh}_t;\overleftarrow{\bh}_t]\in\R^{512}.
\end{align}
A 4-head self-attention layer~\cite{vaswani2017attention} with key-padding mask lets each frame attend across the sequence. Residual LayerNorm outputs $\mathbf a_t$ and validity mask $m_t\in\{0,1\}$ feed masked average pooling to produce the 512-d video representation:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{head}_h&=\softmax\!\left(\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf W^{Q}_h(\mathbf K\mathbf W^{K}_h)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf V\mathbf W^{V}_h,\\
\mathrm{MHA}&=[\mathrm{head}_1;\dots;\mathrm{head}_4]\,\mathbf W^{O},\qquad
\bz=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_t\,\mathbf a_t}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_t}.
\end{align}
A LayerNorm/GELU MLP head (512$\to$256$\to$128$\to$1; dropout 0.5, 0.25) classifies $\bz$. Focal loss~\cite{lin2017focal} down-weights easy samples using $\alpha{=}0.6$, $\gamma{=}2.0$, $p_t^{\mathrm{smooth}}=y(1-\epsilon)+0.5\epsilon$ and $\epsilon{=}0.1$:
\begin{align}
p_t&=\sigm(\hat y)\,y+(1-\sigm(\hat y))(1-y),\qquad \alpha_t=\alpha y+(1-\alpha)(1-y),\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FL}}&=-\mathbb E\!\left[\alpha_t(1-p_t)^{\gamma}\bigl(\tilde y\log\sigm(\hat y)+(1-\tilde y)\log(1-\sigm(\hat y))\bigr)\right].
\end{align}
MixUp~\cite{zhang2018mixup} is applied with probability 0.3 per batch; a cyclic batch shift guarantees non-identity pairing:
\begin{align}
\tilde{\bx}&=\lambda\bx_i+(1-\lambda)\bx_j,\quad \tilde y=\lambda y_i+(1-\lambda)y_j,\quad \lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1.0,1.0),\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mix}}&=\lambda\,\mathcal L(\hat y,y_i)+(1-\lambda)\,\mathcal L(\hat y,y_j).
\end{align}
Stochastic weight averaging (SWA)~\cite{izmailov2018swa} from epoch 30 maintains the running mean
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\theta}_n=\frac{n-1}{n}\,\bar{\boldsymbol\theta}_{n-1}+\frac{1}{n}\,\boldsymbol\theta_n ,
\end{equation}
with the backbone learning rate held at $1/10$ of the base rate. Inference averages five test-time-augmentation (TTA) passes; gradient accumulation over four micro-batches of two videos gives an effective batch of 8. Figure~\ref{fig:eff} reports the training/evaluation curves, while Fig.~\ref{fig:gradcam} provides qualitative examples only.

\section{Stream III: Depthwise-Separable Xception Dual-Branch Network}
Xception~\cite{chollet2017xception} factorises convolution into per-channel depthwise filtering and pointwise $1{\times}1$ mixing, isolating blending-boundary and texture patterns before channel mixing. It yields 2048-d features per frame at $299{\times}299$. ECA-Net~\cite{wang2020eca} reweights pooled channels with a 1D convolution of adaptive kernel size:
\begin{align}
k&=\mathrm{odd}\!\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log_2 C+1}{2}\right\rceil\right),\\
\mathbf g&=\sigm\!\bigl(\mathrm{Conv1d}_k(\mathrm{GAP}(\bF))\bigr),\qquad \hat{\bF}=\mathbf g\odot\bF .
\end{align}
For $C{=}2048$, $k{=}7$. A \emph{parallel learned-feature branch} projects attended features and is fused with the temporal (BiLSTM + MHA) representation. The displayed operation contains no explicit transform in the frequency domain, so it is not described here as a standalone frequency transform:
\begin{align}
\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}&=\mathrm{LN}\bigl(\mathrm{Linear}_{256}(\mathrm{GELU}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathrm{Linear}_{256}(\mathrm{GAP}(\hat{\bF}))))))\in\R^{256},\\
\bz_{\mathrm{fused}}&=[\bz_{\mathrm{temporal}};\,\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}]\in\R^{768},
\end{align}
The classifier head is an MLP (768$\to$256$\to$128$\to$1; dropout 0.3, 0.15). Focal weights are recomputed per fold. From epoch 10, hard-example mining~\cite{shrivastava2016ohem} scores distance to the decision boundary; a class-normalised sampler refreshed every 5 epochs up-samples samples with $h_i\approx1$:
\begin{align}
\alpha_{\mathrm{fold}}&=\frac{N_{\mathrm{real}}}{N_{\mathrm{real}}+N_{\mathrm{fake}}},\\
h_i&=1-2\,|p_i-0.5|.
\end{align}
With probability 0.3, MixUp ($\alpha{=}0.2$) or CutMix~\cite{yun2019cutmix} ($\alpha{=}1.0$) is selected equally often. CutMix simulates blended-face artefacts and encourages local artefact discrimination over global identity cues:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bx}=\mathbf M\odot\bx_i+(1-\mathbf M)\odot\bx_j,\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{adj}}=\frac{\|\mathbf M\|_1}{HW},
\end{equation}
The mixed label is $\tilde y=\lambda_{\mathrm{adj}}y_i+(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{adj}})y_j$; Fig.~\ref{fig:xcep} shows the training and evaluation results.

\section{Stream IV: Hierarchical Swin Transformer with DCT Branch}
Swin-Tiny~\cite{liu2021swin} (patch size 4, $M{=}7$ windows) applies attention within non-overlapping $M{\times}M$ windows, reducing complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(NM^2)$ for $N$ patches. Alternating shifted windows connect boundaries without extra attention cost. Patch merging yields maps at $\tfrac H4{\times}\tfrac W4$, $\tfrac H8{\times}\tfrac W8$, $\tfrac H{16}{\times}\tfrac W{16}$ and $\tfrac H{32}{\times}\tfrac W{32}$, capturing fine-to-coarse cues and border-spanning blending discontinuities. Each block computes
\begin{equation}
\text{W-MSA}(\mathbf Q,\mathbf K,\mathbf V)=\softmax\!\left(\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf K^{\top}}{\sqrt d}+B\right)\mathbf V,
\end{equation}
with a learnable relative position bias $B$. To keep padding from skewing normalisation statistics, padded frames bypass the backbone:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf s_t=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Swin}(\bx_t)&m_t=1,\\ \mathbf 0&m_t=0.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The 768-d output is re-weighted by a fixed-kernel ($k{=}5$) ECA layer and projected to 384-d.

\noindent\textbf{On-the-fly DCT branch.} Unlike Stream~III, this branch analyses raw pixels. A precomputed orthonormal DCT-II basis~\cite{ahmed1974dct} is applied to a bilinearly down-sampled $64{\times}64$ luminance image, with $N=64$, $\alpha_0=\sqrt{1/N}$ and $\alpha_k=\sqrt{2/N}$ for $k>0$:
\begin{align}
D_{k,n}&=\alpha_k\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi k(2n+1)}{2N}\right),\quad k,n\in\{0,\dots,N{-}1\},\\
\mathbf X_{\mathrm{DCT}}&=\mathbf D\,\mathbf I_{\mathrm{gray}}\,\mathbf D^{\top},\quad I_{\mathrm{gray}}=0.299\,R+0.587\,G+0.114\,B.
\end{align}
Log-normalised coefficients, $\hat X_{k,l}=\log(|X_{k,l}|+10^{-6})$, are partitioned into 64 non-overlapping $8{\times}8$ blocks in the full-frame DCT coefficient array (not independent image-space block transforms). Their means $\mu_b$ and standard deviations $\sigma_b$ form
\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_{\mathrm{DCT}}=[\mu_1,\sigma_1,\mu_2,\sigma_2,\ldots,\mu_{64},\sigma_{64}]\in\R^{128},
\end{equation}
A two-layer MLP encodes this as a 192-d frequency representation. Packed sequences keep padding out of the BiLSTM (input 384, hidden 256, 2 layers; Xavier-uniform input, orthogonal recurrent weights, forget-gate bias 1.0). For sample $b$ in batch $B$, the valid length and 704-d fused representation are
\begin{align}
\ell_b&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_{b,t},\qquad\forall b\in[B],\\
\bz&=[\bz_{\mathrm{temporal}}^{512};\,\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}^{192}],
\end{align}
followed by 4-head attention as in Stream~II and an MLP head (704$\to$192$\to$96$\to$1). The training sequence length follows a progressive curriculum (validation always uses 16 frames):
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{train}}(e)=\begin{cases}5&e\in[0,4],\\10&e\in[5,14],\\16&e\ge15.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All five folds are trained and their out-of-fold (OOF) predictions pooled for an unbiased evaluation: per-fold best AUCs are 0.762, 0.836, 0.802, 0.781 and 0.776; mean $0.791\pm0.026$; pooled OOF AUC 0.789 (95\% CI 0.768--0.810; Fig.~\ref{fig:swin}).

\section{Calibrated Late-Fusion Ensemble}
The reported stream coverages are 1,416, 367, 353 and 1,753 videos; the fusion cohort contains 1,520 videos with at least two observed scores. A Bayesian-ridge iterative imputer (20 iterations) completes the partially observed score matrix; the total number of missing cells is not reported because it must be recomputed from the deduplicated score join. The broader MICE framework is described in~\cite{vanbuuren2011mice}; the current description does not establish that multiple completed datasets were generated. Each stream is beta-calibrated~\cite{kull2017beta} using 10-fold out-of-fold (OOF) predictions, with $\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}},\beta_{\mathrm{cal}}\ge0$ and $c\in\R$:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{cal}}=\frac{e^{c}\,p^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}}}{e^{c}\,p^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}}+(1-p)^{\beta_{\mathrm{cal}}}}.
\end{equation}
We compare five strategies on the aligned $N_v\times4$ score matrix: (1)~simple average $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\tfrac14\sum_{i=1}^{4}P_i$; (2)~AUC-weighted average $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}w_iP_i$, where $w_i=\mathrm{AUC}_i/\sum_j\mathrm{AUC}_j$; (3)~rank-based averaging, $r_i(k)=\mathrm{rank}(P_i(k))/N_v$ and $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\tfrac14\sum_i r_i$, which is invariant to calibration differences; (4)~a logistic-regression meta-learner on $[P_{\mathrm{rPPG}},P_{\mathrm{Eff}},P_{\mathrm{Xcep}},P_{\mathrm{Swin}}]$ with 5-fold OOF prediction; and (5)~Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna} with the NSGA-II sampler~\cite{deb2002nsga2}, using 10-fold OOF predictions to maximise AUC and F1 jointly. Normalized-AUC weights provide a reference; this baseline is not Bayesian model averaging. Let $\Delta^{3}=\{\bw\in\R^{4}_{\ge0}:\sum_iw_i=1\}$ be the feasible weight simplex:
\begin{equation}
\bw^{*}\in\operatorname{ParetoArgmax}_{\bw\in\Delta^{3}}\left(\mathrm{AUC}(P_{\bw}),F_1(P_{\bw})\right),\quad P_{\bw}=\sum_iw_iP_i.
\end{equation}
The search selects a 3-model subset (rPPG, EfficientNet, Swin-Tiny) with $w_{\mathrm{rPPG}}{=}0.1063$, $w_{\mathrm{Eff}}{=}0.3435$, $w_{\mathrm{Swin}}{=}0.5501$, excluding Xception (i.e., $w_{\mathrm{Xcep}}=0$). The displayed weights are rounded. With learned temperature $\Tcal{=}0.711$~\cite{guo2017calibration}, the calibrated ensemble score is
\begin{equation}
\hat p=\sigm\!\left(\frac{\logit(\bw^{\top}\bp)}{\Tcal}\right).
\end{equation}
Because $x\mapsto\sigm(\logit(x)/\Tcal)$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1)$, temperature scaling changes calibration but not ranking-based AUC; the weighting determines the ranking gain. The operating threshold is the Youden-J optimum on validation data, $\tau^{*}=0.4377$ (the F1-optimal variant is $\tau^{*}=\argmax_{\tau\in[0.05,0.95]}F_1(\tau)$). Split-conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005conformal} uses $s(x,y)=1-\hat p_y(x)$ and $\mathcal C(x)=\{y:s(x,y)\le\hat q\}$, where $\hat q$ is the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile of calibration scores; under exchangeability, $\Pr\{y\in\mathcal C(x)\}\ge1-\alpha$.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\noindent\textbf{Protocol.} All streams use Eq.~\eqref{eq:leak}, seed 42 and stratified bootstrap 95\% CIs ($n{=}2000$). AUC is primary; accuracy, F1, precision, recall and EER are reported where available. Stream-level thresholds are 0.5 and the ensemble threshold is $\tau^{*}$. Deep streams use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (backbone learning rate one tenth of base), gradient clipping (max-norm 1.0), backbone freezing for epochs 0--4 and a 3-epoch unfreezing ramp. \section{Discussion and Limitations}
Reported score correlations are $r{=}0.232$ (rPPG/Swin-Tiny), $r{=}0.462$ (rPPG/EfficientNet) and $r{=}0.706$ (EfficientNet/Swin). They are consistent with partial complementarity but cannot establish statistical independence; sample counts, uncertainty intervals and the effect of imputed scores are not reported. The reported Xception AUCs are 0.958 (DeepFakeDetection), 0.897 (Deepfakes), 0.685 (FaceSwap) and 0.675 (FaceShifter); subgroup sample sizes and uncertainty are unavailable.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} Four-stream inference is costly. Low-amplitude (0.1--1\%) rPPG can degrade under strong H.264/H.265 compression, occlusion, extreme pose or fewer than 30 clear frames; constant single-segment Welch coherence (1.0) is excluded, leaving fewer than 117 effective features. The effective rPPG sampling rate/duration, provenance of the 1,416 rPPG scores relative to the 80/20/five-fold split, degenerate-CHROM safeguards, probability clipping for beta/logit transforms, Pareto-point selection rule and conformal calibration/test split are undocumented. Physiology-conditioned diffusion may mimic pulse cues. EfficientNet and Xception use single-fold estimates; subgroup counts and uncertainty are absent. The aligned-set fusion result may be optimistic without nested or untouched-test validation. The 1,754-video corpus versus 1,753-video Swin coverage and the missing-score total require reconciliation; per-dataset/class counts, DFDC sampling, custom-corpus provenance/consent and cross-dataset identity resolution are unreported. External generalization and audio remain untested.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
The reported three-stream fusion reaches AUC 0.8081 (95\% CI: 0.7856--0.8293) on the aligned cohort, with a 0.020 gain over Swin-only that is not statistically significant (DeLong $p{=}0.217$). The correlations suggest possible complementarity, not statistical independence. Because the evaluation cohorts differ and the complete fusion-selection pipeline has not been independently validated, the result should be treated as exploratory. Future work includes real-time distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation}, audio-visual fusion, end-to-end rPPG and nested multi-seed cross-dataset tests on diffusion videos.
