\begin{abstract}
Single-modality deepfake-video detectors degrade when synthesis suppresses spatial artefacts or smooths temporal consistency, while subject overlap across splits can confound identity recognition with forgery detection. We present \textbf{NeuroPulse}, a multi-modal forensic architecture integrating four structurally decoupled streams across biophysical, convolutional, channel-attentive, and spectral-transformer subspaces. Stream~I extracts a 117-dimensional, nine-region CHROM-rPPG descriptor for two-level stacked generalisation. Stream~II couples EfficientNet-B4, bidirectional LSTM, and 4-head temporal self-attention. Stream~III combines depthwise-separable Xception, Efficient Channel Attention, a parallel global channel-abstraction branch, and hard-example mining. Stream~IV fuses Swin-Tiny, analytical 64-sub-band 2D block-DCT features, and a packed-sequence BiLSTM. Late fusion unifies Bayesian-ridge iterative imputation, beta calibration, NSGA-II Pareto simplex weighting, temperature scaling, and split-conformal prediction. Across an identity-disjoint 1,754-video corpus (1,042 identities), the Pareto-optimal ensemble reaches AUC 0.8081 (95\% CI: 0.7856--0.8293) and 0.734 F1 with 90.07\% conformal coverage at $\alpha{=}0.10$. Low rPPG--Swin score correlation ($r{=}0.232$) confirms strong cross-modal complementarity.
\keywords{Deepfake detection \and Remote photoplethysmography \and Swin Transformer \and Efficient Channel Attention \and DCT frequency analysis \and Late-fusion ensemble}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
GANs~\cite{goodfellow2014gan}, face swaps, and diffusion models~\cite{ho2020ddpm} enable high-fidelity portrait videos and raise risks of disinformation, identity fraud, and non-consensual imagery~\cite{li2020celebdf,rossler2019ff}. Spatial and temporal artefacts become less reliable under lossy compression and temporal smoothing, whereas physiological signals offer a physics-grounded invariant that spatial generators struggle to maintain coherently~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher}. Subject overlap between training and test splits can also inflate metrics by rewarding biometric recognition over forgery detection.

NeuroPulse decouples feature extraction across four structurally orthogonal forensic pathways: haemodynamic pulse coherence, convolutional spatio-temporal dynamics, depthwise-separable dual-branch channel gating, and hierarchical shifted-window self-attention with analytical DCT statistics. Contributions are: (1)~a unified four-stream multi-modal architecture; (2)~a nine-region, 117-feature CHROM-rPPG descriptor with two-level GBDT stacking; (3)~EfficientNet-B4, Xception, and Swin-Tiny streams with BiLSTM/MHA, ECA, non-identity MixUp/CutMix, hard-example mining, SWA, and a progressive frame curriculum; (4)~a five-stage late-fusion pipeline unifying iterative imputation, beta calibration, NSGA-II Pareto weighting, temperature scaling, and split-conformal prediction; and (5)~strict identity-disjoint group splitting across 1,754 videos and 1,042 identities.

\section{Related Work}
FaceForensics++~\cite{chollet2017xception,rossler2019ff}, Celeb-DF~\cite{li2020celebdf}, and DFDC~\cite{dolhansky2020dfdc} support evaluation of manipulation and compression robustness; DCT frequency artefacts are studied in~\cite{frank2020leveraging}. EfficientNet~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}, Swin~\cite{liu2021swin}, and ECA-Net~\cite{wang2020eca} motivate the visual streams. FakeCatcher~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher}, DeepRhythm~\cite{qi2020deeprhythm}, CHROM~\cite{dehaan2013chrom}, and POS~\cite{wang2017pos} motivate physiological cues. NeuroPulse unifies these complementary modalities via beta/temperature calibration~\cite{guo2017calibration,kull2017beta} and split-conformal uncertainty quantification~\cite{vovk2005conformal}.

\section{Framework, Data and Pre-Processing}
\subsection{Four-Stream Architecture and Complementarity}
NeuroPulse exploits complementary failure modes: compression attenuates spatial artefacts while preserving low-frequency (0.7--4.0\,Hz) pulse phase coherence, whereas high-fidelity rendering may defeat spatial detectors yet lack coherent bilateral rPPG. Each stream $i\in\{1,\dots,4\}$ emits a forgery score $p_i\in[0,1]$ for late fusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Corpus and Identity-Disjoint Evaluation}
The corpus combines FaceForensics++~\cite{rossler2019ff} (C23 originals and six manipulation sets, up to 200 each), Celeb-DF\,v2~\cite{li2020celebdf} (150+50 real, 200 synthesised), a DFDC sample~\cite{dolhansky2020dfdc}, and a custom 400+400 corpus. Balancing, $n_{\mathrm{balanced}}=\min(|\mathcal R|,|\mathcal F|)$, yields 877 real and 877 fake videos (1,754 total) across 1,042 subject identities. Dataset-specific subject keys (actor index, celebrity ID, or metadata key) drive stratified group $K$-fold ($K{=}5$) splits within each dataset, enforcing for fold $q$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\mathrm{ID}^{(q)}_{\mathrm{train}}\cap \mathrm{ID}^{(q)}_{\mathrm{val}}\bigr)=\emptyset ,
\label{eq:leak}
\end{equation}
verified by runtime assertion. All thresholds and calibration parameters are fitted strictly on out-of-fold validation data to measure forgery generalisation rather than identity memorisation.

\subsection{Face Localisation, Alignment and Quality Control}
Stream~I applies MediaPipe FaceMesh~\cite{kartynnik2019facemesh} (468 landmarks) across up to 60 uniform frames; Streams~II--IV apply MTCNN~\cite{zhang2016mtcnn} across $T{=}16$ uniform frames with a centre-crop fallback. For Streams~III--IV, inter-ocular landmarks define roll $\theta=\operatorname{arctan2}(\Delta y_{\mathrm{eyes}},\Delta x_{\mathrm{eyes}})$, de-rotating crops when $|\theta|>2^{\circ}$ to normalise pose. Crops with Laplacian variance $\Var[\nabla^{2}I]<20.0$ are rejected.

\section{Stream I: Haemodynamic rPPG Analysis}
Cardiac blood-volume changes induce dichromatic skin-reflectance fluctuations (rPPG). NeuroPulse models four biophysical invariants: (1)~\emph{spatial coherence}, as the arterial pulse (5--10\,m/s) reaches facial regions with anatomical phase delays disrupted by synthesis; (2)~\emph{harmonic spectral concentration} in the 0.7--4.0\,Hz band; (3)~\emph{autonomic HRV dynamics}; and (4)~\emph{bilateral phase synchronisation}~\cite{lachaux1999plv} across symmetric vascular beds.

Nine convex-hull regions (forehead centre, left/right forehead, cheeks, nose, chin, and jaws) provide high capillary density and bilateral symmetry. For region mask $\Omega_r$ in frame $t$, mean RGB colour is extracted and CHROM~\cite{dehaan2013chrom} decomposes reflected light into orthogonal chrominance channels to suppress specular noise:
\begin{align}
\bar{\mathbf c}^{(r)}_t&=\frac{1}{|\Omega_r|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\Omega_r}\mathbf I_t(x,y),\\
X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}&=3R-2G,\qquad Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}=1.5R+G-1.5B,\\
\mathrm{BVP}(t)&=X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t)-\frac{\sigma_X}{\sigma_Y}\,Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t),
\end{align}
where $R,G,B$ are region-averaged RGB channels and $\sigma_X,\sigma_Y$ are sliding-window standard deviations. In the windowed implementation, mean-normalised signals $R_n,G_n,B_n$ give
\begin{align}
X_s&=3R_n-2G_n,\qquad Y_s=1.5R_n+G_n-1.5B_n,\\
\alpha_c&=\clip\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\sigma(X_s)}{\sigma(Y_s)},\,0,\,10\Bigr),\qquad P=X_s-\alpha_c Y_s.
\end{align}
Window length is $w=\max(\lfloor 1.6\,f_s\rfloor,2)$ and step $s=\max(1,\lfloor w/2\rfloor)$ (50\% overlap), followed by overlap-add reconstruction. The signal is detrended and passed through a 3rd-order Butterworth band-pass~\cite{butterworth1930}:
\begin{equation}
\lvert H(\mathrm{j}\omega)\rvert^{2}=\Bigl[1+\Bigl(\frac{\omega^{2}-\omega_L\omega_H}{(\omega_H-\omega_L)\,\omega}\Bigr)^{2n}\Bigr]^{-1},\qquad n=3,
\end{equation}
with cutoffs $f_L{=}0.7$\,Hz, $f_H{=}4.0$\,Hz (42--240\,BPM). Gaps ${<}30\%$ are linearly interpolated (POS~\cite{wang2017pos} fallback); Welch spectra~\cite{welch1967} use $n_{\mathrm{perseg}}{=}\min(256,\lfloor T/2\rfloor)$ and $n_{\mathrm{fft}}{=}1024$.

The 117-dimensional descriptor includes \textbf{spectral} features (24: SNR, purity, prominence, dominant frequency, harmonic ratio, entropy, centroid, and temporal statistics); \textbf{HRV and phase} (13: eight metrics including RMSSD, SDNN, pNN50, and LF/HF, plus five Hilbert phase/BPM features); \textbf{cross-ROI correlation/coherence} (18); \textbf{geometry} (26 scale-invariant landmark ratios); and \textbf{signal quality/extended bands} (36). Phase synchronisation across region pairs is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PLV}_{ab}=\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}e^{\,\mathrm{j}(\phi_a(t)-\phi_b(t))}\right|,
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{j}^{2}{=}-1$ and $\phi_a(t)$ is the Hilbert phase~\cite{lachaux1999plv}. After winsorisation, scaling, and ExtraTrees gating, XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost} (300 trees), LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} (8 leaves), and HistGradientBoosting (300 iterations) feed an $\ell_2$-logistic meta-learner via 5-fold stacking~\cite{wolpert1992stacking} on an 80/20 group split.

\section{Stream II: EfficientNet-B4 Spatio-Temporal Network}
An ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B4~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} (stochastic-depth rate 0.2; global average pooling) maps each frame $\bx_{b,t}\in\R^{3\times 224\times 224}$ to
\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_{b,t}=\text{EfficientNet-B4}(\bx_{b,t})\in\R^{1792}.
\end{equation}
A 2-layer bidirectional LSTM~\cite{hochreiter1997lstm} models temporal boundary inconsistency across the sequence (256 hidden units per direction; inter-layer dropout 0.5):
\begin{align}
\overrightarrow{\bh}_t&=\mathrm{LSTM}_{\mathrm{fwd}}(\mathbf f_t,\overrightarrow{\bh}_{t-1}),\quad
\overleftarrow{\bh}_t=\mathrm{LSTM}_{\mathrm{bwd}}(\mathbf f_t,\overleftarrow{\bh}_{t+1}),\\
\bh_t&=[\overrightarrow{\bh}_t;\overleftarrow{\bh}_t]\in\R^{512}.
\end{align}
A 4-head self-attention layer~\cite{vaswani2017attention} ($d_k{=}128$) with key-padding mask $m_t\in\{0,1\}$ weights transient manipulation frames. Residual LayerNorm outputs $\mathbf a_t$ feed masked average pooling to produce the 512-d video representation:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{head}_h&=\softmax\!\left(\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf W^{Q}_h(\mathbf K\mathbf W^{K}_h)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf V\mathbf W^{V}_h,\\
\mathrm{MHA}&=[\mathrm{head}_1;\dots;\mathrm{head}_4]\,\mathbf W^{O},\qquad
\bz=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_t\,\mathbf a_t}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_t}.
\end{align}
A LayerNorm/GELU MLP head ($512{\to}256{\to}128{\to}1$; dropout 0.5, 0.25) classifies $\bz$. Focal loss~\cite{lin2017focal} uses $\alpha{=}0.6$, $\gamma{=}2.0$, and label smoothing $\epsilon{=}0.1$. For hard label $y\in\{0,1\}$, probability $p=\sigm(\hat y)$, and smoothed target $y_\epsilon=y(1-\epsilon)+0.5\epsilon$, clipping $p_t^{\mathrm{clip}}=\clip(p_t,10^{-7},1-10^{-7})$ ensures numerical stability:
\begin{align}
p_t&=py+(1-p)(1-y),\quad \alpha_t=\alpha y+(1-\alpha)(1-y),\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FL}}(\hat y,y)&=-\alpha_t(1-p_t^{\mathrm{clip}})^\gamma\bigl[y_\epsilon\log p+(1-y_\epsilon)\log(1-p)\bigr].
\end{align}
MixUp~\cite{zhang2018mixup} is applied with probability 0.3 per minibatch using cyclic non-identity pairing ($\lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1.0,1.0)$) to prevent biometric overfitting:
\begin{align}
\tilde{\bx}&=\lambda\bx_i+(1-\lambda)\bx_j,\quad \tilde y=\lambda y_i+(1-\lambda)y_j,\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mix}}&=\lambda\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FL}}(\hat y,y_i)+(1-\lambda)\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FL}}(\hat y,y_j).
\end{align}
Evaluating $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FL}}$ on hard targets $y_i,y_j$ preserves sharp focal gradients on mixed sequences.

Stochastic weight averaging (SWA)~\cite{izmailov2018swa} from epoch 30 maintains the running mean
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\theta}_n=\frac{n-1}{n}\,\bar{\boldsymbol\theta}_{n-1}+\frac{1}{n}\,\boldsymbol\theta_n ,
\end{equation}
with backbone LR at $0.1\times$ base, 5-pass TTA, and gradient accumulation over $4{\times}2$ micro-batches (effective batch 8; Figs.~\ref{fig:eff} and~\ref{fig:gradcam}).

\section{Stream III: Depthwise-Separable Xception Dual-Branch Network}
Xception~\cite{chollet2017xception} factorises spatial convolution into depthwise filtering and $1{\times}1$ projections, isolating boundary anomalies into $C{=}2048$-d features per $299{\times}299$ crop. ECA-Net~\cite{wang2020eca} recalibrates channels via 1D convolution of adaptive kernel size $k$:
\begin{align}
k&=\mathrm{odd}\!\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log_2 C+1}{2}\right\rceil\right),\\
\mathbf g&=\sigm\!\bigl(\mathrm{Conv1d}_k(\mathrm{GAP}(\bF))\bigr),\qquad \hat{\bF}=\mathbf g\odot\bF .
\end{align}
For $C{=}2048$, $k{=}7$. A parallel \emph{global channel-abstraction branch} projects $\hat{\bF}$ via a LayerNorm--Linear--GELU bottleneck to capture static channel co-occurrence ($\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}\in\R^{256}$) alongside the temporal BiLSTM+MHA pathway ($\bz_{\mathrm{temporal}}\in\R^{512}$):
\begin{align}
\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}&=\mathrm{LN}\bigl(\mathrm{Linear}_{256}(\mathrm{GELU}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathrm{Linear}_{256}(\mathrm{GAP}(\hat{\bF})))))\bigr)\in\R^{256},\\
\bz_{\mathrm{fused}}&=[\bz_{\mathrm{temporal}};\,\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}]\in\R^{768}.
\end{align}
The classifier head is an MLP ($768{\to}256{\to}128{\to}1$; dropout 0.3, 0.15). Focal weights are computed per fold. From epoch 10, online hard-example mining~\cite{shrivastava2016ohem} scores boundary proximity $h_i\in[0,1]$; a class-normalised sampler refreshed every 5 epochs oversamples hard sequences with $h_i\approx1$:
\begin{align}
\alpha_{\mathrm{fold}}&=\frac{N_{\mathrm{real}}}{N_{\mathrm{real}}+N_{\mathrm{fake}}},\\
h_i&=1-2\,|p_i-0.5|.
\end{align}
With probability 0.3, MixUp ($\alpha{=}0.2$) or CutMix~\cite{yun2019cutmix} ($\alpha{=}1.0$) is selected equally often. CutMix splices facial patches across pairs to simulate blending seams and compel local boundary discrimination over global identity context:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bx}=\mathbf M\odot\bx_i+(1-\mathbf M)\odot\bx_j,\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{adj}}=\frac{\|\mathbf M\|_1}{HW}.
\end{equation}
The mixed target is $\tilde y=\lambda_{\mathrm{adj}}y_i+(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{adj}})y_j$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:xcep}).

\section{Stream IV: Hierarchical Swin Transformer with DCT Branch}
Swin-Tiny~\cite{liu2021swin} (patch size 4, $M{=}7$ windows) applies attention within non-overlapping $M{\times}M$ windows, reducing complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(NM^2)$ for $N$ patches. Alternating shifted windows connect boundaries across hierarchical maps at $\tfrac H4{\times}\tfrac W4$, $\tfrac H8{\times}\tfrac W8$, $\tfrac H{16}{\times}\tfrac W{16}$, and $\tfrac H{32}{\times}\tfrac W{32}$, capturing multi-scale cues and border-spanning blending discontinuities. Each block computes
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{W\text{-}MSA}(\mathbf Q,\mathbf K,\mathbf V)=\softmax\!\left(\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf K^{\top}}{\sqrt d}+B\right)\mathbf V,
\end{equation}
with learnable relative position bias $B\in\R^{M^2\times M^2}$. To prevent zero-padding from skewing normalisation statistics, padded frames bypass the backbone:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf s_t=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Swin}(\bx_t)&m_t=1,\\ \mathbf 0&m_t=0.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A fixed-kernel ($k{=}5$) ECA gate reweights the 768-d output before projection to 384-d.

\subsubsection{Analytical 2D Block-DCT Branch.}
To capture frequency-domain synthesis artefacts~\cite{frank2020leveraging}, a precomputed orthonormal DCT-II basis~\cite{ahmed1974dct} transforms a bilinearly down-sampled $64{\times}64$ luminance image ($N{=}64$, $\alpha_0{=}\sqrt{1/N}$, $\alpha_k{=}\sqrt{2/N}$ for $k{>}0$):
\begin{align}
D_{k,n}&=\alpha_k\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi k(2n+1)}{2N}\right),\quad k,n\in\{0,\dots,N{-}1\},\\
\mathbf X_{\mathrm{DCT}}&=\mathbf D\,\mathbf I_{\mathrm{gray}}\,\mathbf D^{\top},\quad I_{\mathrm{gray}}=0.299\,R+0.587\,G+0.114\,B.
\end{align}
Log-normalised coefficients, $\hat X_{k,l}=\log(|X_{k,l}|+10^{-6})$, are partitioned across the $64{\times}64$ spectral array into 64 non-overlapping $8{\times}8$ sub-bands spanning DC to high-frequency grid harmonics. Sub-band means $\mu_b$ and standard deviations $\sigma_b$ form
\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_{\mathrm{DCT}}=[\mu_1,\sigma_1,\mu_2,\sigma_2,\ldots,\mu_{64},\sigma_{64}]\in\R^{128}.
\end{equation}
A two-layer MLP encodes $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{DCT}}$ into a 192-d spectral embedding. Packed sequences (\texttt{pack\_padded\_sequence}) keep padding out of the BiLSTM (input 384, hidden 256, 2 layers; Xavier-uniform input, orthogonal recurrent weights, forget-gate bias 1.0). For sample $b$ in batch $B$, the valid length and 704-d joint representation are
\begin{align}
\ell_b&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_{b,t},\qquad\forall b\in[B],\\
\bz&=[\bz_{\mathrm{temporal}}^{512};\,\bz_{\mathrm{freq}}^{192}],
\end{align}
where 4-head attention pools BiLSTM states before the MLP head ($704{\to}192{\to}96{\to}1$). Training sequence length follows a progressive curriculum (validation uses all 16 frames):
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{train}}(e)=\begin{cases}5&e\in[0,4],\\10&e\in[5,14],\\16&e\ge15.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Across all five identity-disjoint folds, per-fold best AUCs are 0.762, 0.836, 0.802, 0.781, and 0.776 (mean $0.791\pm0.026$), yielding a pooled out-of-fold (OOF) AUC of 0.789 (95\% CI: 0.768--0.810; Fig.~\ref{fig:swin}).

\section{Calibrated Late-Fusion Ensemble}
Streams~I--IV produce OOF probability scores for 1,416, 367, 353, and 1,753 videos respectively (reflecting the 80/20 rPPG split, single-fold deep cohorts, 5-fold OOF evaluation, and quality filtering), forming an aligned fusion cohort of $N_v{=}1,520$ videos with at least two observed scores. Multivariate iterative chained equations~\cite{vanbuuren2011mice} with a Bayesian-ridge estimator (20 iterations) exploit inter-stream covariance to reconstruct the complete $1,520\times 4$ score matrix. Each stream is beta-calibrated~\cite{kull2017beta} using 10-fold OOF predictions, with $\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}},\beta_{\mathrm{cal}}\ge0$ and $c\in\R$:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{cal}}=\frac{e^{c}\,p^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}}}{e^{c}\,p^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}}+(1-p)^{\beta_{\mathrm{cal}}}}.
\end{equation}
On the aligned $N_v\times4$ matrix, we compare (1)~simple average $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\tfrac14\sum_{i=1}^{4}P_i$; (2)~AUC-weighted average $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}w_iP_i$, where $w_i=\mathrm{AUC}_i/\sum_j\mathrm{AUC}_j$; (3)~rank averaging, $r_i(k)=\mathrm{rank}(P_i(k))/N_v$ and $P_{\mathrm{final}}=\tfrac14\sum_i r_i$, invariant to monotonic rescaling; (4)~an $\ell_2$-logistic stacking meta-learner on $[P_{\mathrm{rPPG}},P_{\mathrm{Eff}},P_{\mathrm{Xcep}},P_{\mathrm{Swin}}]$ with 5-fold OOF fitting; and (5)~Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna} with NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2}, jointly maximising AUC and $F_1$ from 10-fold OOF predictions. Let $\Delta^{3}=\{\bw\in\R^{4}_{\ge0}:\sum_iw_i=1\}$; $\mathcal{P}\subset\Delta^{3}$ denotes weights Pareto-optimal for $\mathrm{AUC}(P_{\bw})$ and $F_1(P_{\bw})$:
\begin{equation}
\bw^{*}\in\mathcal{P},\quad P_{\bw}=\sum_iw_iP_i.
\end{equation}
Pareto search selects the sparse three-stream convex combination $w_{\mathrm{Swin}}{=}0.5502$, $w_{\mathrm{Eff}}{=}0.3435$, $w_{\mathrm{rPPG}}{=}0.1063$, and $w_{\mathrm{Xcep}}{=}0.0000$: because EfficientNet-B4 and Swin-Tiny+DCT already span local texture and spectral-spatial cues while CHROM-rPPG supplies an orthogonal physiological signal, simplex sparsity prunes redundant convolutional variance from Stream~III. Logit temperature scaling ($\Tcal{=}0.711$~\cite{guo2017calibration}) yields
\begin{equation}
\hat p=\sigm\!\left(\frac{\logit(\bw^{\top}\bp)}{\Tcal}\right).
\end{equation}
Since $x\mapsto\sigm(\logit(x)/\Tcal)$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1)$, temperature scaling refines probabilistic calibration while preserving the Pareto-optimal AUC ranking. The validation Youden-$J$ threshold is $\tau^{*}_{J}=0.4377$ (with $F_1$-optimal variant $\tau^{*}_{F_1}=\argmax_{\tau\in[0.05,0.95]}F_1(\tau)$). Split-conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005conformal} uses $s(x,y)=1-\hat p_y(x)$ and $\mathcal C(x)=\{y:s(x,y)\le\hat q\}$, where $\hat q$ is the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ calibration quantile; under exchangeability, $\Pr\{y\in\mathcal C(x)\}\ge1-\alpha$.

\subsubsection{Results.}
Tables~\ref{tab:individual} and~\ref{tab:ablation} summarise stream and fusion performance; on the aligned cohort ($n{=}1,520$), every probability-space fusion method outperforms the Swin-only baseline (0.788 AUC). Optuna NSGA-II + $\Tcal{=}0.711$ achieves the best metrics: AUC reaches \textbf{0.8081} ($+0.0201$ gain; 95\% CI: $[0.786, 0.829]$), accuracy \textbf{72.9\%} $[70.6, 75.1]$, F1 \textbf{0.734} $[0.710, 0.755]$, precision \textbf{0.693} $[0.670, 0.718]$, and recall \textbf{0.779} $[0.746, 0.809]$. Paired tests against Swin-only give DeLong $z{=}1.235$ ($p{=}0.217$)~\cite{delong1988} and continuity-corrected McNemar $\chi^2{=}0.570$ ($b{=}47,c{=}39,p{=}0.450$)~\cite{mcnemar1947}. At $\alpha{=}0.10$, split-conformal prediction achieves \textbf{90.07\%} coverage (target $\ge 90.0\%$), routing 40.3\% of uncertain videos to $\mathcal C(x){=}\{0,1\}$ for audit.

\section{Architectural Complexity and Cross-Stream Synergy}
Calibrated stream scores correlate at $r{=}0.232$ (rPPG/Swin-Tiny), $r{=}0.462$ (rPPG/EfficientNet-B4), and $r{=}0.706$ (EfficientNet-B4/Swin-Tiny), confirming that narrowband (0.7--4.0\,Hz) chrominance phase coherence and $O(NM^2)$ shifted-window spatial-spectral tokens yield largely decorrelated residuals. Across manipulation families, Stream~III (Xception+ECA) excels on full-scene synthesis (DeepFakeDetection AUC 0.958) and autoencoder Deepfakes (0.897) via depthwise-separable boundary isolation, whereas graphics-based FaceSwap (0.685) and occlusion-aware FaceShifter (0.675) preserve skin texture and rely on Stream~I's pulse coherence and Stream~IV's global attention.

\subsubsection{Computational Complexity and Trade-Offs.}
NeuroPulse balances forensic depth with modular efficiency. Stream~I runs 468-vertex FaceMesh tracking, 9-ROI averaging, $O(T\log T)$ FFT Welch periodograms, and GBDT stacking on CPU, requiring $\ge 30$ clear frames and moderate bitrate to preserve low-amplitude ($0.1\text{--}1.0\%$) skin chrominance oscillations. Streams~II ($224{\times}224$, 1792-d) and III ($299{\times}299$, 2048-d) scale linearly with $T{=}16$, with 4-head attention adding $O(T^2 d_k)$ cost ($T^2{=}256$). Stream~IV bounds spatial attention to $O(NM^2)$ ($M{=}7$) while computing $64{\times}64$ orthonormal DCT-II projections ($\mathbf D\mathbf I_{\mathrm{gray}}\mathbf D^{\top}$) and packed BiLSTM recurrence on GPU. Since NSGA-II Pareto optimisation assigns $w_{\mathrm{Xcep}}{=}0$, deployment executes only Streams~I, II, and IV, cutting GPU compute by ${\sim}28\%$ while retaining the 0.8081 AUC.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
NeuroPulse unifies nine-region CHROM-rPPG haemodynamic analysis, EfficientNet-B4 spatio-temporal attention, depthwise-separable Xception dual-branch channel gating, and Swin-Tiny shifted-window transformers with analytical 2D block-DCT statistics. Via Bayesian-ridge iterative imputation, OOF beta calibration, Optuna NSGA-II Pareto simplex weighting, temperature scaling ($\Tcal{=}0.711$), and split-conformal prediction, the ensemble reaches 0.8081 AUC (95\% CI: 0.7856--0.8293), 0.734 F1, and 90.07\% conformal coverage at $\alpha{=}0.10$. Future work explores knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation}, audio-visual fusion, neural rPPG, and physiology-conditioned diffusion benchmarks.
