\begin{abstract}
The rapid proliferation of deepfake video technology poses a critical
threat to digital trust, demanding robust and generalizable detection
systems. We present \textit{NeuroPulse}, a multi-modal detection
framework comprising four complementary streams with model-specific
training protocols: (1) a remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) classifier
using a 117-dimensional physiological core augmented with 96 frequency-domain
and 36 geometry features (249 dimensions in the final ML input) and an ML
stacking ensemble; (2) an
EfficientNet-B4 spatio-temporal CNN with bidirectional LSTM and
multi-head self-attention; (3) a dual-branch Xception architecture with
ECA channel attention and a parallel learned feature branch; and (4) a
Swin-Tiny Transformer backbone with an on-the-fly discrete cosine
transform (DCT) branch and a \texttt{pack\_padded\_sequence}-based
BiLSTM. The CNN streams use identity-aware grouping, focal training,
model-specific augmentation and stochastic weight averaging where
applicable, while Swin-Tiny is evaluated using five-fold out-of-fold
prediction. A late-fusion ensemble combines the three selected
streams---rPPG, EfficientNet-B4 and Swin-Tiny---after score imputation
and calibration, with ensemble weights obtained by Optuna TPE
optimisation. The ensemble achieves an AUC-ROC of \textbf{0.8081}
(95\% CI: 0.7856--0.8293) and an F1-score of 0.7335 on a 1,520-video
fusion cohort evaluated using 10-fold OOF predictions, spanning
FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF v2, DFDC, and a custom
dataset --- yielding an AUC gain of 0.0197 over the best individual
stream (Swin-Tiny, AUC=0.7884).

\keywords{Deepfake detection \and rPPG \and EfficientNet \and Xception
\and Swin Transformer \and BiLSTM \and multi-head attention \and focal
loss \and ensemble learning \and DCT frequency analysis}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) and diffusion-based video
synthesis methods have enabled the creation of highly convincing
face-swap and face-reenactment videos --- collectively known as
\textit{deepfakes} --- at negligible cost~\cite{rossler2019ff}. Their
misuse in political disinformation, non-consensual imagery, and financial
fraud has generated significant societal harm and accelerated research
into automated detection.

Existing deepfake detectors broadly fall into two families.
\textit{Spatial detectors} identify per-frame artifacts introduced by
synthesis networks: blending boundaries, spectral anomalies, texture
inconsistencies, and GAN fingerprints~\cite{chollet2017xception}.
\textit{Temporal detectors} model physiological or motion signals across
video frames: heartbeat-induced colour variation
(rPPG)~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher}, blink patterns, and
micro-expression consistency. Neither family alone generalises across all
manipulation types and compression qualities.

We address this limitation with \textbf{NeuroPulse}, a four-stream
framework capturing spatial, spatio-temporal, frequency, and
physiological cues. Our main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A 117-dimensional rPPG physiological core (CHROM method across
    9 ROIs, capturing spectral, HRV, phase, and geometry markers), augmented
    with 96 frequency-domain and 36 geometry features to form the 249-dimensional
    final ML input, with a sanitisation, train-only percentile clipping,
    zero-variance removal, RobustScaler and ExtraTrees feature-selection
    pipeline followed by a three-model ML stacking ensemble (XGBoost,
    LightGBM, HistGradBoost), achieving AUC = 0.628.
    \item An EfficientNet-B4 + BiLSTM + MHA model with Focal Loss
    ($\alpha{=}0.6$, $\gamma{=}2.0$), SWA, and 5-pass TTA, achieving
    standalone AUC = 0.7390.
    \item An Xception dual-branch model with ECA-Net channel attention
    and a 256-dim parallel learned feature branch, achieving
    AUC = 0.7848.
    \item A Swin Transformer Tiny model with on-the-fly 128-dim DCT
    features, \texttt{pack\_padded\_sequence} BiLSTM, and progressive
    frame curriculum, achieving OOF AUC = 0.7891.
    \item An Optuna TPE-optimised ensemble with temperature
    calibration, MICE imputation, beta-calibration, and conformal
    prediction, achieving \textbf{AUC = 0.8081}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent\textbf{Spatial CNN methods.} Rossler et al.~\cite{rossler2019ff}
demonstrated that Xception fine-tuned on FaceForensics++ achieves strong
detection at C23 compression but degrades at high compression.
MesoNet~\cite{afchar2018mesonet} uses a compact CNN targeting mesoscopic
properties. Li et al.~\cite{li2020celeb} proposed Celeb-DF as a harder
benchmark, revealing significant performance drops for existing methods.

\noindent\textbf{Frequency-domain detectors.} Frank et
al.~\cite{frank2020leveraging} showed that GAN artifacts are visible in
the DCT frequency spectrum. Several works exploit DFT and DCT features
without relying purely on spatial appearance.

\noindent\textbf{Physiological signal detectors.}
FakeCatcher~\cite{ciftci2020fakecatcher} pioneered rPPG-based detection,
exploiting the fact that GAN synthesis fails to preserve
photoplethysmographic colour variation. DeepRhythm~\cite{qi2020deeprhythm}
used CNN-extracted rPPG maps for the same purpose.

\noindent\textbf{Transformers and attention.} The Swin
Transformer~\cite{liu2021swin} introduced hierarchical shifted-window
attention for efficient visual processing. ECA-Net~\cite{wang2020eca}
provides a lightweight 1D convolution alternative to SE-Net channel
attention. Ensemble methods have been applied in video forensics to
combine complementary detectors; in this work the ensemble weights are
optimised with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna}.

\section{System Architecture}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The pipeline proceeds as follows. Face extraction is cached to disk as
\texttt{.npy} arrays to eliminate redundant computation. The
physiological stream (Model 1) extracts up to 60 frames uniformly
sampled via \texttt{np.linspace} using MediaPipe
FaceMesh~\cite{lugaresi2019mediapipe}, while the spatial-temporal
streams (Models 2--4) use MTCNN~\cite{zhang2016mtcnn} to extract up to
16 uniformly sampled frames; Swin-Tiny additionally uses a progressive
5$\to$10$\to$16 frame curriculum during training. Face preprocessing is
model-specific:
EfficientNet-B4 uses MTCNN crops resized to $224{\times}224$ with a
margin of 40 and \emph{no} eye-landmark rotational alignment; Xception
and Swin-Tiny use MTCNN with landmark-based eye alignment and
blur-quality handling, falling back to centre cropping when the
detection-quality conditions are not met.

Each model stream produces a scalar fake probability $p_i \in [0,1]$.
MICE imputes missing scores, after which beta-calibration aligns score
scales. The three-model subset (rPPG, EfficientNet, Swin-Tiny),
excluding Xception from the final fusion, was selected by comparing
closed-form AUC-weighted fusion strategies, and the ensemble weights
were then optimised with Optuna TPE. The calibrated ensemble computes:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{p} = \text{sigmoid}\!\left(\frac{\text{logit}(\mathbf{w}^\top
  \mathbf{p})}{T}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}$ are the Optuna-TPE-optimised weights and
$T{=}0.7109$ is the learned temperature scalar.

\section{Model 1: rPPG Physiological Stream}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Feature Extraction}

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) extracts the blood volume pulse (BVP)
signal from facial colour variation caused by cardiac activity. We
extract up to 60 frames via MediaPipe FaceMesh (468 landmarks) across 9
facial ROIs to compute a 117-dimensional feature vector.

\noindent\textbf{Missing-signal handling.} For each ROI signal, if the
missing fraction is below 30\% the missing samples are linearly
interpolated; if the missing fraction is 30\% or higher, the ROI signal
is rejected (invalidated). The resulting signals are subsequently
numerically sanitised.

\noindent\textbf{CHROM BVP Extraction.} The CHROM
method~\cite{dezwart2013chrom} decomposes skin-reflected light into
chrominance channels to suppress specular noise:
\begin{equation}
  X_{\mathrm{CHROM}} = 3R - 2G, \quad
  Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}} = 1.5R + G - 1.5B
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BVP}(t) = X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t) - \alpha\,
  Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}(t), \quad
  \alpha = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_X}{\sigma_Y},
  0, 10\right)
\end{equation}
where $R, G, B$ are spatially averaged colour channels per ROI, and
$\sigma_X, \sigma_Y$ are the standard deviations of $X_{\mathrm{CHROM}}$
and $Y_{\mathrm{CHROM}}$ within each window. The signal is processed in
1.6-s windows with 50\% overlap; the RGB channels are mean-normalised
within each window, and the final BVP is reconstructed by overlap-add of
the windowed estimates.
A third-order zero-phase Butterworth band-pass filter with cut-off
frequencies 0.7 and 4.0\,Hz is implemented using forward-backward
filtering:
\begin{equation}
  (b,a) = \operatorname{Butterworth}\!\left(3,
  \left[\frac{0.7}{f_N},\,\frac{4.0}{f_N}\right]\right), \qquad
  s_f = \operatorname{filtfilt}(b,a,s)
\end{equation}
where $f_N$ is the Nyquist frequency and $s$ is the raw BVP signal. The
passband corresponds to an HR range of 42--240 BPM.

\noindent\textbf{117-Dimensional Feature Space.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spectral (24):} 12 features for each of the two
    principal ROIs: Welch's method~\cite{welch1967} SNR, spectral
    purity, peak prominence, dominant frequency, harmonic ratio,
    entropy, centroid, MAD, standard deviation, zero-crossing rate,
    kurtosis, and skewness ($12\times2=24$).
    \item \textbf{HRV (8):} RMSSD, SDNN, pNN50, pNN20, LF power
    (0.04--0.15\,Hz), HF power (0.15--0.40\,Hz), LF/HF ratio, and total
    power.
    \item \textbf{Cross-ROI correlation (12):} 12 cross-ROI
    correlation measures.
    \item \textbf{Coherence (6):} six cross-ROI coherence features.
    \item \textbf{Phase/BPM (5):} 5 phase and BPM features computed
    from FFT/cross-spectral phase.
    \item \textbf{Geometry (26):} Scale-invariant metrics from 468
    facial landmarks.
    \item \textbf{Extended signal/band/quality features (36):} eight
    sub-groups: 5 signal-quality features, 9 multiband-power features,
    5 cross-region BPM features, 5 temporal-stability features,
    4 skin-reflection features, 3 SNR-dispersion features,
    3 phase-synchronisation (PLV) features, and 2 RGB-correlation
    features ($5+9+5+5+4+3+3+2=36$).
\end{itemize}
Together these give $24+8+12+6+5+26+36=117$ features.

The Welch spectral estimates use the following parameters, where $T$ is
the number of samples in the signal:
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{perseg}} = \min\!\left(256,\ \max\!\left(4,\
  \lfloor T/2\rfloor\right)\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{overlap}} = \min\!\left(128,\
  \lfloor n_{\mathrm{perseg}}/2\rfloor\right), \qquad
  n_{\mathrm{fft}} = 1024
\end{equation}

Phase synchronisation across ROI pairs is computed in the frequency
domain from the FFT of the ROI signals $X_i(f)$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{ij}(f) = \arg\!\left(X_i(f)\,X_j^{*}(f)\right),
  \qquad f\in[0.7,\,4.0]\ \mathrm{Hz}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PLV}_{ij} = \left| \frac{1}{N_f} \sum_{f}
  e^{j\phi_{ij}(f)} \right|
\end{equation}
where $N_f$ is the number of frequency bins in the band. The phase is
obtained from the FFT/cross-spectral procedure of our implementation; it
is \emph{not} derived from a Hilbert-transform output.

\noindent\textbf{Final ML feature input (249-D).} The 117-dimensional
vector above is the rPPG physiological core. For the executed stacking
classifier, this core is augmented with 96 frequency-domain features
(32 FFT-mean, 16 FFT-standard-deviation, 32 DCT-mean, and
16 DCT-standard-deviation features) and 36 geometry features (26
geometry-mean and 10 geometry-standard-deviation features), yielding a
249-dimensional final ML input:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ML}} = \left[\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{rPPG}}^{117};\;
  \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{freq}}^{96};\;
  \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{36}\right] \in \mathbb{R}^{249}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary 2560-dimensional EfficientNet-B0 feature block is not
included in the final classifier input.

\subsection{ML Stacking Classifier}

The 249-dimensional feature input $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ML}}$
(117-dimensional rPPG core plus the 96 frequency-domain and 36 geometry
features) is classified by a stacking ensemble (v7.2). The
preprocessing and classification pipeline is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item large-value sanitisation / log transform of the input
    features;
    \item train-only 1st--99th percentile clipping;
    \item zero-variance feature elimination;
    \item \texttt{RobustScaler}, followed by clipping of the scaled
    values to $[-10,10]$;
    \item \texttt{ExtraTrees} feature selector (250 trees) with a
    threshold of $1.2\times$ the mean importance;
    \item base estimators: XGBoost ($n_{\mathrm{estimators}}{=}300$,
    max\_depth${=}3$, learning rate $0.02$, subsample $0.8$,
    colsample\_bytree $0.8$, $L_1{=}1$, $L_2{=}10$); LightGBM
    ($n_{\mathrm{estimators}}{=}300$, max\_depth${=}3$,
    num\_leaves${=}8$, learning rate $0.02$, subsample and feature
    fraction $0.8$, $L_1{=}1$, $L_2{=}10$); HistGradientBoosting
    (max\_iter${=}300$, max\_depth${=}5$, max\_leaf\_nodes${=}15$,
    learning rate $0.02$, $L_2{=}5$);
    \item a Logistic Regression meta-learner (balanced class weighting,
    max\_iter${=}1000$) trained with internal 5-fold stacking.
\end{enumerate}
Training uses identity-aware GroupShuffleSplit (80/20). On the
identity-aware held-out split of 331 videos, the standalone rPPG
stacking classifier achieved AUC = 0.628, Accuracy = 0.5921, and
F1 = 0.6110. On the fusion cohort the ensemble achieves
AUC-ROC = 0.6283, Accuracy = 59.9\%, and F1 = 0.618.

\section{Model 2: EfficientNet-B4 Spatio-Temporal CNN}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Architecture}

The EfficientNet-B4 backbone~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} is loaded from
\texttt{timm} (\texttt{drop\_path\_rate=0.2}, \texttt{global\_pool=avg}),
producing a 1792-dim spatial feature per frame:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f}_{b,t} = \text{EfficientNet-B4}(\mathbf{x}_{b,t})
  \in \mathbb{R}^{1792}
\end{equation}

The 1792-dim sequence feeds a 2-layer bidirectional LSTM:
\begin{align}
  \overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t &= \text{LSTM}_{\text{fwd}}(\mathbf{f}_t,
  \overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t-1}) \\
  \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t &= \text{LSTM}_{\text{bwd}}(\mathbf{f}_t,
  \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t+1}) \\
  \mathbf{h}_t &= [\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t;
  \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{512}
\end{align}
with hidden size 256 per direction and inter-layer dropout $p{=}0.5$.

4-head Multi-Head Self-Attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention} with
key-padding mask and attention dropout $p{=}0.5$, followed by dropout, a
residual connection and layer normalisation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A} = \text{MHA}_{p=0.5}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}),
  \quad \text{MHA}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}) =
  \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}
  \right)\mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A}' = \text{LN}\!\left(\mathbf{X} +
  \text{Dropout}_{0.5}(\mathbf{A})\right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{X}$ is the BiLSTM output sequence. Masked average pooling
produces a 512-dim video representation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} m_t \,\mathbf{a}'_t}
  {\sum_{t=1}^{T} m_t}
\end{equation}
where $m_t \in \{0,1\}$ is the frame validity mask. The classifier head:
Linear(512$\to$256) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.5) $\to$
Linear(256$\to$128) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.25) $\to$
Linear(128$\to$1).

\subsection{Focal Loss with Label Smoothing}

Following focal loss~\cite{lin2017focal}, with $p=\sigma(\hat{y})$:
\begin{equation}
  p_t = y\,p + (1-y)(1-p), \qquad
  \alpha_t = \alpha\,y + (1-\alpha)(1-y)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  y_s = y(1-\epsilon) + 0.5\,\epsilon
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FL}} = \alpha_t (1-p_t)^\gamma\,
  \operatorname{BCEWithLogits}(\hat{y}, y_s)
  \label{eq:focal}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha{=}0.6$, $\gamma{=}2.0$, and $\epsilon{=}0.1$.

\subsection{MixUp Augmentation}

MixUp~\cite{zhang2018mixup} is applied with probability 0.3 per training
batch, with $\lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1.0,1.0)$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \lambda \mathbf{x}_i + (1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_j,
  \quad \lambda \sim \mathrm{Beta}(1.0, 1.0)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mix}} = \lambda\,\mathcal{L}(\hat{y}, y_i) +
  (1-\lambda)\,\mathcal{L}(\hat{y}, y_j)
\end{equation}
\texttt{torch.roll} with \texttt{shifts=1} guarantees a non-identity
permutation at batch size 2.

\subsection{Augmentation Pipeline and Optimisation}

In addition to MixUp, the executed training pipeline applies the
following image augmentations (probability $p$ in parentheses):
HorizontalFlip (0.5), ShiftScaleRotate (0.3), RandomBrightnessContrast
(0.5), HueSaturationValue (0.3), RGBShift (0.3), ImageCompression
(0.2), Downscale (0.2), GaussNoise (0.3), GaussianBlur (0.2), ISONoise
(0.2), CoarseDropout (0.3), and Posterize (0.1).

Optimisation uses AdamW with learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, weight
decay $5\times10^{-4}$, a physical batch size of 2 and gradient
accumulation of 4 (effective batch size 8), and gradient clipping at 1.0.
The cosine warm-up/decay learning-rate schedule is the HuggingFace
\texttt{get\_cosine\_schedule\_with\_warmup} scheduler, with warm-up
ratio 0.1, stepped after each optimizer update (i.e. after gradient
accumulation). With $S$ total optimizer-update steps and $W=0.1S$
warm-up steps, its learning-rate factor is
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(s) = \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{s}{W}, & s < W \\[6pt]
  \dfrac{1+\cos\left(\pi\dfrac{s-W}{S-W}\right)}{2},
  & W \le s < S.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The backbone is frozen for epochs 0--4 and unfrozen at epoch 5 with a
backbone learning rate of $\mathrm{LR}/10$. SWA starts at epoch 30 and uses
per-parameter-group SWA learning rates to preserve the backbone/head ratio:
$\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{SWA,backbone}}{=}5\times10^{-6}$ and
$\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{SWA,other}}{=}5\times10^{-5}$, with five SWA
annealing epochs (the backbone remains at a 10:1 lower learning rate than
the other groups). Early stopping uses an AUC patience of 25 epochs and a
validation-loss patience of 15 epochs.

At test time, 5-pass TTA is used: (1) the original image, (2) a
horizontal flip, (3) brightness $+15$, (4) brightness $-15$, and
(5) Gaussian blur with a $3\times3$ kernel; the five predictions are
uniformly averaged.

\subsection{Stochastic Weight Averaging and Checkpoint Selection}

SWA~\cite{izmailov2018swa} is attempted from epoch 30:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_n = \frac{n-1}{n}\,
  \bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n-1} + \frac{1}{n}\,\boldsymbol{\theta}_n
\end{equation}
The backbone LR is held at $1/10$ of the base rate. The reported model
is the \emph{best checkpoint} (\texttt{best\_cnn\_model\_fold0.pth}),
reached at epoch 31 with best validation AUC = 0.7394; this is distinct
from the SWA checkpoint. Final metrics of the loaded best checkpoint
(95\% CIs in brackets): AUC = 0.7390 [0.6838, 0.7857],
Accuracy = 0.6921 [0.6431, 0.7358], F1 = 0.6762 [0.6144, 0.7283],
EER = 0.3015 [0.2664, 0.3641].

\subsection{Grad-CAM Visualisation}

Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} activation maps are computed on the
EfficientNet-B4 \texttt{conv\_head} layer. For class score $y^c$ and
feature maps $A^k$:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i,j}
  \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{ij}^k}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{GradCAM}}^c = \operatorname{ReLU}\!\left(
  \sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k\right)
\end{equation}

\section{Model 3: Xception Dual-Branch Architecture}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Architecture}

The \texttt{legacy\_xception} backbone~\cite{chollet2017xception} from
\texttt{timm} produces 2048-dim spatially pooled features
$\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{2048}$. ECA-Net~\cite{wang2020eca} channel
attention re-weights features before temporal modelling. The kernel
size follows the rule
\begin{equation}
  k_0 = \left\lfloor \left|\frac{\log_2 C + b}{\gamma}\right|
  \right\rfloor, \qquad
  k = \begin{cases} k_0, & k_0 \text{ odd} \\ k_0 + 1, & k_0 \text{ even}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $b{=}1$ and $\gamma{=}2$, and the channel re-weighting is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{g} = \sigma\!\left(\text{Conv1d}_k(\mathbf{F})\right),
  \quad \hat{\mathbf{F}} = \mathbf{g} \odot \mathbf{F}
\end{equation}
For $C{=}2048$, $k{=}7$. In the temporal pathway, the ECA-weighted
per-frame features undergo an input projection
$2048\to512\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.15), followed by a 2-layer
bidirectional LSTM (hidden size 256 per direction) that gives a 512-dim
representation $\mathbf{H}$ per frame. Dropout(0.3) is applied after the
BiLSTM, before 4-head multi-head self-attention (attention dropout 0.15)
with a residual connection and layer normalisation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A}' = \text{LN}\!\left(\mathbf{H} +
  \text{MHA}(\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H})\right)
\end{equation}
Masked temporal pooling then yields the 512-dim temporal representation
$\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{temporal}}$.
A \textbf{parallel learned feature branch},
motivated by complementary compression-related representations,
operates on the ECA-weighted representation (it does not perform an
explicit FFT/DCT transform); it is
$2048\to256\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.15) $\to256\to$ LN:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{par}} = \text{LN}(\text{Linear}_{256}(
  \text{Dropout}_{0.15}(\text{GELU}(\text{LN}(\text{Linear}_{256}(
  \hat{\mathbf{F}})))))) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
\end{equation}
Dual-stream fusion concatenates temporal and parallel-branch
representations:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{fused}} = [\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{temporal}};\;
  \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{par}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{768}
\end{equation}
The classifier: Linear(768$\to$256) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$
Dropout(0.3) $\to$ Linear(256$\to$128) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$
Dropout(0.15) $\to$ Linear(128$\to$1).

The Xception focal loss uses the same binary focal-loss structure as
Eq.~(\ref{eq:focal}), with the fold-specific $\alpha_{\mathrm{fold}}$
and $\gamma=2.0$, and label smoothing $\epsilon=0.05$:
\begin{equation}
  p_t = y p + (1-y)(1-p), \qquad
  \alpha_t = \alpha_{\mathrm{fold}} y +
  (1-\alpha_{\mathrm{fold}})(1-y)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  y_s = y(1-\epsilon) + 0.5\epsilon
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Xception}} = \alpha_t(1-p_t)^\gamma
  \operatorname{BCEWithLogits}(\hat{y},y_s).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamic Focal Alpha and Hard Negative Mining}

For the executed fold-0 experiment, the focal-loss $\alpha$ is computed
from the training-class distribution as:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{\mathrm{fold}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{real}}}{N_{\mathrm{real}}
  + N_{\mathrm{fake}}}
\end{equation}
Hard negative mining activates at epoch 10 and is refreshed every 5
epochs, with sample hardness:
\begin{equation}
  w_i = 1 - 2\left|p_i - 0.5\right|
\end{equation}
Samples near the decision boundary ($w_i \approx 1$) are up-sampled; the
normalisation is class-balanced so that minority examples are not
disproportionately oversampled.

\subsection{MixUp + CutMix Curriculum}

The augmentation is activated with probability 0.5 per batch. Within the
activated batches, MixUp ($\lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(0.2,0.2)$) and
CutMix~\cite{yun2019cutmix} ($\alpha{=}1.0$) are selected with equal
probability. For MixUp:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \lambda\mathbf{x}_i +
  (1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_j, \qquad
  \lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(0.2,0.2)
\end{equation}
For CutMix:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{M} \odot \mathbf{x}_i +
  (1-\mathbf{M}) \odot \mathbf{x}_j, \quad
  \lambda_{\mathrm{adj}} = 1 - \frac{|B|}{HW}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}$ is a binary mask that is 0 inside the randomly
sampled cut box $B$ and 1 elsewhere. The model achieves
AUC = 0.7848 (95\% bootstrap CI [0.7341, 0.8310]), AP = 0.7598,
Accuracy = 0.7139, F1 = 0.6892, Precision = 0.7089, Recall = 0.6707, and
EER = 0.2688. The per-manipulation AUCs are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:manip}. Per-manipulation AUC estimates
are based on small subgroup sample sizes and should therefore be
interpreted descriptively.

\subsection{Optimisation and Unfreezing}

The backbone is frozen for epochs 0--4 and is unfrozen at the
configured epoch 5. The executed code then applies a three-epoch
backbone learning-rate ramp over internal epoch indices
$e\in\{5,6,7\}$:
\begin{equation}
  r_e = \frac{e-5}{3}, \qquad
  \mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{backbone}}(e) = \frac{\mathrm{LR}}{100} +
  r_e\left(\frac{\mathrm{LR}}{10} - \frac{\mathrm{LR}}{100}\right),
  \quad e\in\{5,6,7\}.
\end{equation}
With $\mathrm{LR}{=}10^{-4}$, this gives
$10^{-6}$, $4\times10^{-6}$, and $7\times10^{-6}$ for internal
epochs 5, 6, and 7, respectively; the explicit ramp is inactive after
epoch 7. Training uses AdamW with weight decay $10^{-2}$, a physical
batch size of 2 and gradient accumulation of 8 (effective batch size 16).
The scheduler is \texttt{CosineAnnealingLR} with $T_{\max}{=}15$ and
$\eta_{\min}{=}0.01\times10^{-4}{=}10^{-6}$, stepped per epoch (not
per optimizer step). SWA uses $\mathrm{SWA\_LR}=5\times10^{-5}$ with
three annealing epochs.

Inputs are normalised with $\mu=\sigma=[0.5,0.5,0.5]$. The executed
training transforms are listed in Table~\ref{tab:xc_aug}. At test time,

\section{Model 4: Swin Transformer with DCT Branch}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Architecture}

The \texttt{swin\_tiny\_patch4\_window7\_224}
backbone~\cite{liu2021swin} (patch size 4, window size 7,
\texttt{drop\_path\_rate=0.2}) yields
768-dim spatial features via hierarchical shifted-window attention:
\begin{equation}
  \text{W-MSA}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}) =
  \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d}}
  + B\right)\mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is a learnable relative position bias. Padding frames are
skipped during backbone computation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{s}_t = \begin{cases}
  \text{Swin}(\mathbf{x}_t) & m_t = 1 \\
  \mathbf{0} & m_t = 0
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The 768-dim per-frame features are projected to 384 dimensions and fed
to a bidirectional LSTM producing a 512-dim representation per frame,
followed by 4-head multi-head self-attention (attention dropout 0.15,
i.e.\ half of the model dropout of 0.3), a residual connection with
layer normalisation, and masked average pooling. The resulting 512-dim
temporal vector is concatenated with the 192-dim DCT representation
($512+192=704$).

\subsection{Training Configuration}

The Swin stream uses ECA channel attention whose kernel size follows
the same rule as in the Xception stream,
\begin{equation}
  k_0 = \left\lfloor \left|\frac{\log_2 C + b}{\gamma}\right|
  \right\rfloor, \qquad
  k = \begin{cases} k_0, & k_0 \text{ odd} \\ k_0 + 1, & k_0 \text{ even}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $b{=}1$, $\gamma{=}2$; for $C{=}768$ this gives
$k_{\mathrm{ECA}}{=}5$. The classifier hidden dimension is 192, and
early-stopping patience is 10. Training uses AdamW with learning rate
$10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, physical batch size 2, and gradient
accumulation of 4 (effective batch size 8). MixUp is activated from epoch 10, with
$\lambda\sim\mathrm{Beta}(0.4,0.4)$; Swin uses MixUp only and does not
use the Xception MixUp/CutMix alternation.
The loss uses the same implementation structure as the focal loss in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:focal}), with Swin-specific parameters
$\alpha{=}0.5$, $\gamma{=}2.0$, and label smoothing $\epsilon{=}0.08$.

Before SWA, the learning rate follows a \texttt{LambdaLR} schedule with
warm-up ratio 0.1 and a minimum LR ratio of 0.1:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(s) = \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{s}{W}, & s < W \\[6pt]
  0.1 + 0.9\cdot\dfrac{1+\cos(\pi p)}{2}, & s \ge W
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the step index, $S$ is the total number of steps before
SWA, $W=0.1S$ is the warm-up length, and
$p=(s-W)/(S-W)$ is the normalised progress after warm-up. SWA starts at
epoch 15 with
per-group SWA learning rates that preserve the backbone/head LR ratio
($10^{-6}$ for the backbone groups and $10^{-5}$ for the other groups),
five annealing epochs, and cosine annealing.
At test time, 6-pass TTA is used: (1) the original image, (2) a
horizontal flip, (3) brightness scaling by $1.15$, (4) brightness scaling
by $0.85$, (5) Gaussian blur with a $3\times3$ kernel, and (6) a $93\%$
centre crop followed by resize; the predictions are uniformly averaged.

\subsection{On-the-Fly DCT Frequency Branch}

A 2D DCT matrix is pre-computed once as a \texttt{register\_buffer}:
\begin{equation}
  D_{k,n} = c_k \cos\!\left(
  \frac{\pi k(2n+1)}{2N}\right), \quad k,n \in [0,N)
\end{equation}
with orthonormal scaling
\begin{equation}
  c_0 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}}, \qquad c_k = \sqrt{\frac{2}{N}},\;\; k>0
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{DCT}} = \mathbf{D}\, \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{gray}}\,
  \mathbf{D}^\top
\end{equation}
Because the Swin input frames are ImageNet-normalised, the DCT branch
first reverses that normalisation using the stored ImageNet mean and
standard deviation. The resulting image is converted to grayscale and
bilinearly downsampled to 64$\times$64:
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{gray}} = 0.299\,R + 0.587\,G + 0.114\,B
\end{equation}
Log-magnitude normalisation: $\hat{X}_{k,l} = \log(|X_{k,l}| +
10^{-6})$. The 64$\times$64 matrix is divided into 64 non-overlapping
8$\times$8 blocks; per-block statistics form a 128-dim feature vector:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{DCT}} = [\mu_{1}, \ldots, \mu_{64},
  \sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{64}] \in \mathbb{R}^{128}
\end{equation}
A two-layer MLP encodes this to 192-dim frequency representation.

\subsection{pack\_padded\_sequence BiLSTM}

Variable-length sequences are packed for clean gradient flow:
\begin{equation}
  \ell_b = \sum_{t=1}^{T} m_{b,t}, \quad \forall b \in [B]
\end{equation}
LSTM weight initialisation: Xavier uniform for $\mathbf{W}_{ih}$,
orthogonal for $\mathbf{W}_{hh}$, forget-gate bias $= 1.0$.
The 704-dim fused classifier input:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z} = [\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{temporal}}^{512};\;
  \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{freq}}^{192}]
\end{equation}
followed by Linear(704$\to$192) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.3)
$\to$ Linear(192$\to$96) $\to$ LN $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout(0.15)
$\to$ Linear(96$\to$1).

Table~\ref{tab:frames}. OOF AUC across folds:
0.7624, 0.8364, 0.8019, 0.7805, 0.7761; Mean = $0.7914 \pm 0.0258$.
Combined OOF AUC = 0.7891 (95\% CI: 0.7683--0.8097, 1,000 bootstrap

\subsection{Face Preprocessing and Identity-Aware Splits}

Face preprocessing is model-specific. EfficientNet-B4 uses MTCNN crops
at $224{\times}224$ with margin 40 and no rotational alignment.
Xception and Swin-Tiny use MTCNN with landmark-based eye alignment: the
tilt angle $\theta = \arctan2(\Delta y, \Delta x)$ is corrected by affine
rotation when $|\theta| > 2^\circ$. A Laplacian blur-quality check
(threshold = 20.0) is applied, and fallback / centre-crop behaviour is
used when the detection-quality conditions are not met. Faces are cached
as per-video \texttt{.npy} arrays.

StratifiedGroupKFold (5 folds) with identity as the group key prevents
data leakage. Identity overlap ($\texttt{train\_ids} \cap
\texttt{val\_ids} = \emptyset$) and video overlap were checked under the
corresponding group-splitting procedures across 1,042 unique identities.

\subsection{Common Training Details}

The CNN/transformer streams use AdamW, gradient clipping
(max\_norm=1.0), and gradient accumulation. The backbone is frozen for
epochs 0--4 and unfrozen at epoch 5, with model-specific unfreezing
learning-rate schedules: EfficientNet-B4 uses $\text{LR}/10$ after
unfreezing; Xception sets the backbone to $\text{LR}/100$ at unfreezing
and applies the executed three-epoch ramp described in Section~6;
Swin-Tiny uses $\text{LR}/10$. SWA begins at epoch 15 (Xception, Swin) or epoch 30
(EfficientNet). Test-time augmentation uses 5 passes for EfficientNet-B4
and 6 passes for Xception and Swin-Tiny. Evaluation protocols are
model-specific: the rPPG stream uses an identity-aware
GroupShuffleSplit; EfficientNet-B4 and Xception are single-fold
experiments (fold 0); Swin-Tiny is evaluated with the full five-fold
out-of-fold protocol. All models were trained on Kaggle P100 GPUs.

\section{Ensemble Fusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Score Imputation and Calibration}

The four streams cover different video subsets (1,416; 367; 353; 1,753
videos). After merging, 1,520 videos have at least two scores. MICE
(IterativeImputer with BayesianRidge, 20 iterations)~\cite{vanbuuren2011mice}
imputes 2,507 of the 6,080 ($1520\times4$) model-score cells (41.2\%).
MICE is fit once on the complete merged 1,520-video fusion cohort before
the downstream 10-fold OOF procedures; therefore, the reported fusion
result is an internal OOF evaluation rather than a fully nested outer-test
estimate.
Beta-calibration~\cite{kull2017beta} is applied per stream using 10-fold
OOF, where beta calibration outperformed isotonic calibration for all
four streams:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{cal}} = \sigma\!\left(a\ln p + b\ln(1-p) + c\right)
\end{equation}
where $a,b,c$ are fitted using OOF calibration.

\subsection{Subset Selection and Ensemble Weight Optimisation}

All final fusion OOF evaluations and the Optuna weight optimisation use
the same cached 10-fold splits on the 1,520-video fusion cohort.

\noindent\textbf{Subset selection.} The three-model subset was selected
by comparing AUC-weighted closed-form fusion strategies: Strategy A
(all four models) AUC = 0.8039; Strategy B (rPPG + EfficientNet + Swin)
AUC = 0.8044; Strategy C (rPPG + Swin) AUC = 0.7917; Strategy D (Swin
only) AUC = 0.7884. Strategy B was chosen.

\noindent\textbf{BMA baseline.} Bayesian model averaging was evaluated
as a closed-form baseline; its weights, computed from mean
log-likelihood, are $(0.3103,\,0.3428,\,0.3470)$ and give AUC = 0.8034.

\noindent\textbf{Final weights.} Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna} TPE
single-objective optimisation (1,500 trials, 10-fold OOF, objective:
maximise AUC) selected the final ensemble weights (AUC = 0.8081). An
alternative NSGA-II multi-objective configuration (600 trials; objectives:
AUC and log-loss) reached AUC = 0.8080 and was not selected. The optimal
weights are
$w_{\mathrm{rPPG}}{=}0.1063$, $w_{\mathrm{Eff}}{=}0.3435$,
$w_{\mathrm{Swin}}{=}0.5501$. The weighted score is
\begin{equation}
  p_w = 0.10634\,p_{\mathrm{rPPG}} + 0.34352\,p_{\mathrm{Eff}}
  + 0.55014\,p_{\mathrm{Swin}}
\end{equation}
and the final ensemble score and decision are
\begin{equation}
  \hat{p} = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\text{logit}(p_w)}{T}\right),
  \quad T = 0.7109
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \begin{cases} 1, & \hat{p} \ge 0.4377 \\
  0, & \hat{p} < 0.4377 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The Youden-J optimal decision threshold is 0.4377 (the F2-optimal
threshold is 0.1600).

\section{Discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent\textbf{Model diversity matters.} The pairwise correlation
between rPPG and Swin-Tiny predictions ($r{=}0.232$) is substantially
lower than EfficientNet-Swin ($r{=}0.706$), confirming that the
physiological stream provides complementary signal. Despite its lower
individual AUC, rPPG's low correlation contributes measurably to
ensemble diversity.

\noindent\textbf{Identity leakage is critical.} Random splits that allow
the same identity to appear in both training and validation can produce
overly optimistic estimates in face-centric datasets. Our experiments
therefore use identity-aware grouping for the corresponding model
streams, and identity and video overlap were checked under the
corresponding group-splitting procedures across 1,042 unique identities.

\noindent\textbf{Complementary learned features.} Xception's
per-manipulation breakdown shows high AUC for DeepFakeDetection (0.958)
and Deepfakes (0.897), but lower AUC for FaceSwap (0.685) and
FaceShifter (0.675). The parallel learned feature branch provides a
complementary representation alongside the ECA-weighted temporal
features; however, no dedicated ablation isolates its effect on
compressed-video detection, and the subgroup AUCs are based on small
samples and are descriptive only.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} The rPPG stream requires sufficient valid
sampled frames to construct approximately two seconds of signal and is
sensitive to heavy occlusion, failed face detection, and extreme pose.
Cross-dataset generalisation (e.g., DF40, WildDeepfake) remains
unexplored. The final ensemble evaluation is an OOF evaluation on the
merged cohort but does not constitute a strictly nested outer test of
every model-selection stage. Training was constrained to under 12 hours
per model on Kaggle P100 GPUs; the effect of longer training schedules
was not evaluated.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We have presented NeuroPulse, a multi-modal deepfake detection
framework fusing rPPG physiological signals, spatio-temporal CNN
features, frequency-domain cues, and hierarchical vision transformer
representations. The framework combines heterogeneous physiological,
CNN-based and transformer-based detectors through model-specific
identity-aware training and evaluation protocols and late fusion. With
MICE imputation, beta-calibration, and Optuna ensemble optimisation, the
system achieves an AUC-ROC of 0.8081 on the 1,520-video fusion cohort
under the implemented 10-fold out-of-fold evaluation. Future
work will incorporate cross-dataset evaluation, DF40 generalisation
testing, and end-to-end rPPG CNN encoding.
